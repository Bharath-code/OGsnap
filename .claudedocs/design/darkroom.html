Diagnosis

Why the current design reads as AI slop

It isn't one bad choice. Three problems stack: the page talks to the wrong person, every section uses the same template, and nothing in the visuals comes from the product's own world.

Two audiences in one page

  • The hero sells to Lovable founders. Four sections later we show a 3-line route handler and "Framework coverage".
  • The FAQ is titled "for search + AI answers" and talks about "render pipelines".
  • The Twitter meta still says "OG images in 3 lines. Every modern JavaScript framework."
  • docs/06 and docs/07 still describe a dark, indigo, developer-first brand.

One template, six times

  • Each section is a Badge eyebrow, a serif H2, a paragraph and a grid of cards.
  • Feature grids of icon + title + two lines of copy, which every SaaS template ships.
  • The same Reveal fade-up on every block, so nothing stands out.
  • Numbered "01 02 03" cards that read as decoration.

Nothing from the subject

  • Cream gradient, blurred blobs and dot grain are generic textures.
  • Cormorant Garamond plus Work Sans says "boutique", which has nothing to do with links or sharing.
  • The product is about how a link looks inside other apps, yet no chat, feed or Slack message appears on the page.
  • The dashboard has seven equal tabs, and three of them are for an API this audience never uses.

Concept

A darkroom for links

"Snap" is already a camera word. A link preview is a small photograph of your site that crawlers take when someone shares it. Without an og:image the photo comes out blank. OGSnap develops it. That gives us a villain (the grey box), a hero moment (developing), and a vocabulary: develop, snap, print, frame, contact sheet.

Principle

We are the frame. You are the picture.

OGSnap's own chrome is ink, paper and fog. Saturated color appears only inside previews, and it is always the customer's brand. That makes every customer's site the most colorful thing on our page.

Principle

One motion: develop

Grey → blurred → sharp color, with a soft flash at the end. We use it for every "your preview is ready" moment on the landing page, in onboarding and in the install check. Nothing else animates to draw attention.

Principle

Show the feed, not features

Every claim appears inside a message, post or Slack thread, because that is where the customer sees the problem. We don't use icon grids.

Principle

Talk like a founder friend

No "pipelines", "SDK" or "render events" on public pages. Say "your link", "your site", "paste this". Developer terms go into a single Developer page inside the app.

A sitemap that draws itself, with each page getting its own card and title. This is the upgrade story for per-page previews. Copy must stay honest: the site-wide image works on every platform, and per-page cards need a custom domain on Cloudflare (T10).

6 · Questions a founder asks

Do I need to code?

No. You paste two lines into your site's settings. On Lovable, you paste one prompt and it makes the change for you.

Where do the colors come from?

From your live site: the logo, main colors and heading font. You can change any of them before you install.

What happens to my current preview image?

You replace it. If you already have a good one for a page, keep it and we skip that page.

Why does LinkedIn still show the old image?

LinkedIn keeps previews for about a week. Paste your link into their Post Inspector to refresh it now. The app gives you the direct link.

I run an agency. Can each client keep their own brand?

Yes. The $49 plan covers 10 sites, and each has its own logo, colors and font.

Can I cancel?

Anytime, from Billing. Your site goes back to its old preview at the end of the month.

FAQ copy is a draft. The Post Inspector and cancellation answers describe intended behavior. Confirm both before shipping (Phase 0 rule: no false claims).

Build plan

No new dependencies. Landing first.

Everything here uses CSS, inline SVG and the existing Reveal IntersectionObserver. Landing work comes first because the day-30 gate depends on outreach traffic converting on that page. The app redesign can wait until the first paying sites exist.

  1. Tokens, fonts, logo

    Swap the palette to Paper/Print/Ink/Fog/Flash. Load Archivo with its wdth axis, Geist and Geist Mono via next/font. Delete the body gradients, .bg-grain, Cormorant and Work Sans.

    globals.css · tailwind.config.ts · app/layout.tsx · ~0.5 day

  2. Four primitives

    <Aperture> (SVG plus 20 lines of math), <Develop> (a class toggle and CSS), <OgCard>, and <PlatformFrame variant="x|linkedin|slack|imessage|whatsapp|discord">. Stop using Badge as an eyebrow.

    components/brand/aperture.tsx · components/ui/develop.tsx · components/preview/* · ~0.5 day

  3. Landing rewrite

    Hero composer and chat (restyled LiveRenderDemo, same stream), grey wall, three frames, sitemap, prints, FAQ, wordmark footer. Delete the SDK, framework and trust-signal sections. Fix metadata and JSON-LD copy.

    app/page.tsx · components/onboarding/live-render-demo.tsx · ~1.5 days

  4. Checker page

    /check gets the same hero and uses the log component from the install check.

    app/check/* · ~0.5 day

  5. App shell and Sites

    Rail nav with three items. Keys, Renders and Playground move to /dashboard/developer. Sites becomes rows plus a detail view with the preview wall and install check.

    dashboard/layout.tsx · dashboard-nav.tsx · dashboard/sites/page.tsx · ~1.5 days

  6. Retire the old brand docs

    Replace docs/06 and docs/07 with a link to this spec. That follows the plan's "no new docs" rule and removes the stale indigo guidance.

    docs/06-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md · docs/07-BRAND.md · 10 min

Open decisions. (1) The plan leaves the name open for the no-code audience. This direction works with any name, but the aperture and "snap" language assume we keep OGSnap. (2) Should the chat in the hero show a real customer's site once one exists? That would be stronger than the fictional Tidepool. (3) Platform frames stay generic and don't copy any platform's real UI or logos.
